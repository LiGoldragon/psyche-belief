# Moon (Candra)

A painting, not a diagram. Every part of it has the quality of Candra, and nothing in it has another quality: the subject, the setting, the things, the format, the composition, the light, the colour, the stroke and the surface. It holds no word, no letter, no numeral and no glyph. Where the correspondence below gives two readings of one thing, both are present in the one picture.

## The painting

- Early night. A waxing moon over water, and its light on a riverbank beside a house with a well.
- A woman, beautiful, round in outline yet lean, with fine black curling hair, wide clear eyes and a gentle, attentive face. She wears clean white robes and pearls and holds an infant at her breast.
- Around her are the things of the household: a vessel of milk, rice, salt, a conch, a white parasol, a silver bowl, new cloth drying.
- A hare in the grass, a cow with her calf, a crane standing in the shallows, a partridge; white flowers, creepers heavy with sap, herbs, a tree that gives milky sap.
- The river runs out to the sea, where an island lies; a small boat is on the water.
- The moon is seen in the sky and again, changed and broken, in the water: growing in one, thinner in the other.

## Format, composition and light

- A wide sheet, wider than tall. Nothing stands on a centre line: the composition flows in curves from the upper left, where the moon is, down and across to the woman and out along the water.
- The light is cold, silvery white, moist, and carries a faint green in it; a little warmth, borrowed from the sun, glows on the bright side of the moon and on the woman's face.
- Rain-season air: mist, dew, wet ground.

## Manner

- Watercolour and ink wash on wet paper: the colour runs and settles, edges are soft and bleed, much of the white is the paper itself.
- Colours: white, silver, pearl, pale green, the black of her hair and of the water at night.

## The correspondence, whole

### nature

Candra is the moistening light of night and the power of water. Its figure is feminine. It stands for the mind as it feels and responds to changing impressions. The light does not hold a single form: its growth and decline matter to how it acts. The Moon’s strength belongs to the night, especially its earlier part.

The northwest, the rainy season, the taste of salt and the acts of swallowing and drinking belong here. Infancy is its time in a human life. Life at that age grows quickly through nourishment and depends on the person who provides it. The Moon can darken in an eclipse when Earth comes between it and the Sun.

**Cold and borrowed warmth.** In a thermal classification, *mṛdavaśca śitā* gives cold as the property of the gentle group, including the Moon. In the account of physical powers the Moon chiefly acts to moisten, *ὑγραίνειν*, and has a little warming action, *ἠρέμα καὶ τοῦ θερμαίνειν*, from solar illumination.

**Benefic and adverse by phase.** The general roster calls the Moon one of the *śubhā*, benefics. A phase-sensitive list places the *kṣīṇendu*, waning Moon, among the *pāpā*, adverse powers. The latter has a phase condition.

**Three qualities.** One formal classification calls Moon and Venus *rājasikaḥ*, of rajas, the quality associated with activity or passion. Another assigns the Moon to *satva*, or sattva, the quality associated with clarity or goodness; a portrait calls it *sāttvikaḥ*, “of that quality.” These are different classifications of the Moon, not inferences from its gentle behavior.

**Directions and measures of strength.** In chart directional strength the Moon and Venus take the north, *sitacandrau tathottare*, linked with the fourth chart place. One rule of motional strength gives the Sun and Moon strength northward, *punaruttareṇa*; a separate rule of course gives Moon and Saturn *yāmyaṃ*, the southern course. A second course reading gives strength south of the equator in English. The chart quarter establishes neither rule of motion.

### character

Candra is gentle and receptive. Soft speech, a kindly manner, playfulness and patience go with the ability to take in what is happening around it. Receptivity can become adaptability or fickleness. As the light grows it can bring a more open and resolute temper; as it fails, a quieter and more cautious one.

The mother is its closest human likeness, especially in a night birth. The nurse and mistress of the household extend the same relation: the person who feeds, shelters and keeps watch over others. Lovers and young women belong to another side of its responsiveness.

**Moon at the bends and crossings.** At the Moon’s north or south bend, *ἐν μὲν ταῖς καμπίαις*, the human mind is described as adaptable, resourceful and changeable; the bends are limits of latitude. With the Moon instead at either crossing, *ἐπὶ δὲ τῶν συνδέσμων*, the mind is sharper, more active and more readily stirred. This concerns the Moon’s placement and the human mind.

### body

The lunar figure is beautiful, round in outline but lean, with fine curling hair, clear wide eyes and a melodious voice. Its constitution is full of wind and phlegm. Blood is its chief tissue. In the human body Candra belongs especially to the stomach, abdomen, womb and breasts, and to nourishment and rapid growth. The face, left eye and left side also come under it. Breathing, marrow and the membranes around the brain are further associations. A mark on the left side of the head is a further bodily association.

### occupations

Candra can hold royal rank. Away from the court it governs the work of sustaining life: cultivating the land, washing cloth and caring for a household. It also belongs to poets and those skilled in sacrifice. Divination is a more particular calling: when the Moon rules action and is separating from a conjunction with Mercury, the reading depends on its sign and can reach ritual, prophecy or the interpretation of dreams.

### things

Water and the things handled every day come first: milk, food, salt, oil and ghee; cloth, new garments and clean white robes. A conch, glass, a white fan or fly whisk and a parasol belong among its objects. Its actions include receiving and spending household goods, conceiving and keeping house. The rhythm can move beyond the home into assembling people, sailing or spending time abroad. Bathing, giving, anointing, making jewels and preparing for a festival suit its gentler forms of activity.

### places

Look first to wells, riverbanks and other places where water is present. The house and household form its inhabited center; the ocean and its islands extend the setting beyond it. A place associated with women or a sanctuary of Pārvatī is also lunar. Watery forts and places of herbs, honey or liquor are more particular settings.

### animals and plants

Flowers, rice, herbs, fruit and watery creatures recur. Among plants, creeping or sap-filled growth and trees that give a milky sap make the connection with moisture plain. The hare, cow, antelope, crane and partridge share the field with other animals active at night or at home near water. Grain and seed belong to the nourishment the Moon sustains.

### metals and stones

Pearls, silver and gems are the leading materials. Silver can be uncoined; bell metal is also associated with Candra.

### colours

**Lunar hue.** An assigned lunar colour is *sita*, white, and a planetary list gives *śvetaka*, whitish or silvery in appearance. Another portrait gives *πράσινος*, green, for the Moon’s own colour. Black hair belongs to the figure, a different feature.

### illnesses

Fluid accumulation, especially dropsy, fits the Moon’s association with bodily moisture. The left eye belongs to its anatomy, while loss of sight is an outcome only in certain adverse alignments involving the Moon and other bodies.

**Moon in an approaching alignment.** If the Sun and Moon are together or opposite and the lights and harmful planets at the chart’s angles approach one another, the Moon at either crossing or at either bend can intensify disorders of posture or movement.
