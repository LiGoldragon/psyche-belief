# Mars (Maṅgala)

## The painting

- Night, in the south, in summer heat. A young warrior at a forge that stands on a battlefield below a fort.
- He is compact and strong, with firm limbs, a lean waist, short shining curled red hair and fierce, bloodshot eyes. His face is clear, noble and resolved, and the dark around him is thick with smoke.
- He draws a blade of heated iron from the fire with one hand and holds a commander's standard in the other; he wears a red garment, singed and partly burnt, coral at his neck, copper and gold on his arms.
- Weapons lie stacked; the hearth burns hard; behind him grain stores are on fire and men fight.
- A ram and a cock stand by him; a jackal and a vulture wait at the edge; thorn trees, and red lotuses in a ditch that reflects the fire.
- He is in the middle of the stroke: the act already begun.

## Format, composition and light

- A tall canvas cut by one great diagonal from the lower left to the upper right, along his arm and the blade; everything leans and drives along it.
- The only light is fire: blood-red and white-hot at the iron, falling off fast into black.

## Manner

- Thick oil laid on with the knife: slashed, fast, rough strokes left as they fell, paint standing in ridges, sparks flicked on.
- Colours: blood red, black, iron grey, copper, sparks of gold.

## The correspondence, whole

### nature

Maṅgala is fire as heat, dryness and the power to burn. It is masculine, malefic and strong by night. It represents vigor brought to bear: the strength that can begin a hard act, overcome resistance or carry force too far. Its divine likeness is the youthful warrior Skanda. It belongs to the south as a quarter, to summer and to the span of a day and night. Its taste is bitter or sharply biting. Mature life, when strength is available for strenuous work, is its human time.

**Three qualities and active vigor.** In one three-quality classification Mars is grouped under *sattvādhikā*, predominantly sattva, the quality associated with clarity or goodness; in another it goes with *tamāṃsi*, tamas, the quality associated with dullness or inertia. In a separate map of cosmic functions *sattvaṃ kujo* assigns Mars vigor, the capacity for a strenuous act. The function leaves the formal classification open.

### character

Maṅgala is brave, ardent, quick to act and quick to anger. It would rather undertake difficult work than live without a contest. In a favorable position it can mean nobility, willingness to take risks, command and generosity. It can speak plainly and do what it says. The same speed can turn to recklessness, the steadiness to obstinacy and command to cruelty. In a poor position it is prone to violence, disturbance and acts that shorten the time available for thought. A brother, especially a younger one, is a close human likeness; kindred who stand beside one another and enemies who test one another represent different relations to its force.

### body

The figure is youthful, compact and strong, with firm limbs, a lean waist and fierce eyes. The hair can be short, shiny and curled. Bile is its prevailing constitution and marrow its characteristic tissue. Its human bodily associations include the blood and veins, kidneys, left ear, genitals and the rear parts of the body. A mark on the right side of the back is another particular sign. Accounts of injuries to a person do not supply further features of the figure.

### occupations

The soldier and general come first, with armed attendants and officials who command others. It also belongs to people whose work meets hard resistance: smiths, quarry workers, builders in wood and stone, and artisans working metal. When Mars aspects the Sun, its fire can be the heat used by cooks, smiths and metal molders, or in cautery. With Mercury it can appear in the handwork of armorers, sculptors and surgeons. The trade changes with the partner; the force brought to the work is recognizably martial.

**Heated metal and command.** In the natal-ruler reading, *ferro ignito*, heated iron, describes work with fire and iron. The same description calls the natives *principalia semper sibi vindicantes potestatis insignia*, always claiming for themselves the insignia of chief authority. This describes the native’s claim to rank, distinct from the craft and from the planet’s fire.

### things

Weapons, fire, and red, singed or partly burnt garments are immediate associations. Wine, unrefined sugar and products dug from the earth are also present. Its undertakings include battle, resistance, hunting, seizing goods and acts of daring. When the power is unrestrained it brings raids, quarrels, burning and sudden loss. Drought and fire in grain stores give its heat a public consequence. Command itself is a rank or capacity, separate from the objects a commander may possess.

### places

A fireplace or hearth is first, then the kitchen or a place where crafts use fire. Battlefields, forts, mines and strongholds give its energy larger settings. The ground underfoot also belongs to it. Ujjain and the Avanti country belong to its geography, along with major rivers and mountain foothills.

### animals and plants

Red flowers and fruits, pulses and thorny trees belong here. The red lotus is a particular flower; pungent or bitter trees are another association. Its animals include ram, cock, jackal, monkey and vulture, with elephants and quarry taken in the hunt also in its field.

### metals and stones

Gold is well established, alongside copper and copper ore. Iron belongs to the side of Maṅgala that works hard matter. Coral is its most distinctive nonmetallic material.

### colours

**Planetary fire and natal features.** The planet itself is *sanguinolentum*, bloodlike or bloody in its fiery brilliance, in a description that says the fires moderate with Saturn or its domicile. In a separate natal judgment under Mars as ruler, *rubeis capillis* and *oculis sanguinolentis* are red hair and bloodshot eyes on the person.

### illnesses

Fever, burning, cuts, wounds and loss of blood are foremost. Ulcers, boils and inflamed skin continue the association with heat and irritation. With Mercury involved, blows or burns in an athletic contest or violent attack can be linked with loss of sight. Mercury can also increase the severity of dry and ulcerating conditions. Other outcomes have narrower premises: one link to severe debility by day requires an angular Mars and a Moon turned away from it and unjoined to any planet. The conditions matter because strength by itself is not illness.
