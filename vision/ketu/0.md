# Ketu, a painting: the source

A painting, not a diagram. Every part of it has the quality of Ketu, and nothing in it has another quality: the subject, the setting, the things, the format, the composition, the light, the colour, the stroke and the surface. It holds no word, no letter, no numeral and no glyph. Where the correspondence below gives two readings of one thing, both are present in the one picture.

## The painting

- Desert frontier at dusk in the southwest, with a hill fort on a far ridge. A comet stands in the sky, a fiery apparition with a long tail of smoke; the full moon near it is darkened by eclipse.
- A figure stands high on a rock, elevated and lean, an ascetic bearing a weapon, with a fierce red gaze and a venomous tongue, breathing smoke. Its body has a smoky brilliance and is thinning away into the air at its edges.
- It wears rags, mixed cloth full of holes. It tips a mud vessel, and what pours out is seed, which the wind carries over the ground; where seed has fallen, a few green shoots stand.
- At its feet an anthill and a dark snake hole, a serpent, a clump of thorn bushes; a donkey, a ram, a wolf, a camel and an owl at a distance; insects in the air; horse gram.
- One blue stone with a line of light across it lies on the rock.

## Format, composition and light

- A wide canvas that empties toward one side: forms are whole at the left and come apart toward the right, where the ground is left bare.
- Ash-pale, smoky light, with three points of red fire: the gaze, the head of the comet, the eclipsed moon.

## Manner

- Paint scraped back and rubbed away, thin and broken, the weave and the bare ground showing through; scattered, separate touches, like things let go.
- Colours: ash grey, smoke, bone white, dull earth, points of red, one blue.

## The correspondence, whole

### nature

The figure is malefic and associated with the southwest and with touch.

**Crossing, eclipse myth and release.** In orbital geometry Ketu is the southward crossing: after describing the northward passage, the account says “the opposite occurs at the south node.” “Swallowed up” belongs to the eclipse myth at the nodal axis. In the symbolic account Ketu is the “Point of release”; what has been assimilated can leave as waste or be passed on as seed, and spontaneous giving can help others at a cost to the giver.

### character

Ketu is fearsome and can be harsh. It turns toward what has already been acquired or inherited. Holding to it can become inertia; letting it go can help bring two sides of a life together.

**Moon at the bends and crossings.** At the Moon’s north or south bend, *ἐν μὲν ταῖς καμπίαις*, the human mind is described as adaptable, resourceful and changeable; the bends are limits of latitude. With the Moon instead at either crossing, *ἐπὶ δὲ τῶν συνδέσμων*, the mind is sharper, more active and more readily stirred. This concerns the Moon’s placement and the human mind.

### body

The figure is elevated and lean, with a fierce gaze, a venomous tongue and a windy constitution.

### occupations

The snake catcher and soldier belong here, alongside forms of religious life.

### things

A mud vessel, mixed cloth, rags and a garment with holes. The figure can bear arms and breathe smoke.

### places

An anthill, a dark snake hole or a forest.

**Mundane allotment, cometary usage and natal crossing.** A mundane allotment gives Ketu *giridurga*, hill forts, *maru*, deserts, and *pratyanta*, frontier districts. The name also denotes a fiery apparition, *keturūpam*, in a separate account of comets and portents. The allotment does not itself identify its Ketu with the natal crossing or expressly name a comet.

### animals and plants

Horse gram, donkey, ram, wolf, camel, serpent and owl. Insects and a clump of trees or bushes also belong.

### metals and stones

**Identity of the gemstone.** One planetary allotment names the *vaidūryake* half of *gomedhavaidūryake* for Ketu; it is identified as lapis lazuli or turquoise in one reading and cat’s eye in another. A separate list gives *nīlamaṇiḥ ketor*, Ketu’s blue stone, without naming a species.

### colours

**Smoke and gaze.** The figure has *dhūmradyutir*, smoky brilliance, and *raktogradṛṣṭi*, a fierce red gaze. The descriptions concern different features of the same portrait.

### illnesses

Bruised limbs belong to the figure’s affliction associations.

**Moon in an approaching alignment.** If the Sun and Moon are together or opposite and the lights and harmful planets at the chart’s angles approach one another, the Moon at either crossing or at either bend can intensify disorders of posture or movement.
