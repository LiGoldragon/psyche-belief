# Sūrya, a painting: the source

A painting, not a diagram. Every aspect of it, subject, format, composition, light, colour, stroke, surface, air and mood, has the quality of Sūrya, and nothing in it has another quality. It holds no word, no letter, no glyph, no border ornament and no second focus. Citations name the corpus and text in the symbology context.

## Subject

- One figure alone: the golden person within the sun, "golden altogether to the tips of his nails", with golden beard and hair (upanishad-samkhya: Chāndogya 1.6.6).
- A king, a man in his prime, square-limbed and broad, standing upright and still (parashara-yavana: Yavanajātaka 1.123; ptolemy: Tetrabiblos IV.10).
- Honey-tawny eyes, wide open, looking straight out at the viewer: the all-seeing eye (varahamihira: Bṛhajjātaka 2.8; orphic: Hymn to Helios).
- A lotus in each of his two hands, held at the shoulders (purana-graha: Matsya 94.1).
- A diadem, ruby earrings, a long necklace; a coat of mail; clothed in red from breast to feet (varahamihira: Bṛhat Saṃhitā 57.46-48; citra-rasa: Viṣṇudharmottara 67).
- He stands on a red lotus upon a car with one wheel, drawn by seven horses abreast, facing forward (purana-graha: Matsya 94.1; Garuḍa 1.39.6).
- A lion at rest at his feet (parashara-yavana: Yavanajātaka 1.116).

## Format and composition

- A square canvas (varahamihira: Bṛhajjātaka 2.8, caturasra).
- Strict symmetry about the vertical middle; the figure exactly at the centre and frontal; the centre of his chest is the centre of the canvas (purana-graha: Matsya 93.11, he stands in the middle).
- Behind him a perfect, unbroken disc, wide and large (varahamihira: Bṛhat Saṃhitā 3.39).
- Everything else is arranged on straight lines that leave the centre: the rays, the seven horses fanned evenly, the folds of cloth.
- Twelve chief rays, evenly spaced, long, distinct and clean, reaching every edge (purana-graha: Agni, the twelve Ādityas; varahamihira: Bṛhat Saṃhitā 3.39).
- Nothing equals him in size or brightness; he fills the height.

## Light

- He is the only source of light. Nothing casts a shadow on him; every shadow in the painting falls straight away from the centre.
- Brightest at the centre, never dimming to darkness: the edges are still lit.
- Noon, in the hot dry season; a cloudless sky (ptolemy: Tetrabiblos I.4, heat and dryness).

## Colour

- Molten gold and heated copper for the body and disc; vermilion and saffron for cloth; ruby red in the jewels; the yellow of lotus pollen at the heart of the disc (parashara-yavana: Yavanajātaka 1.120, 1.123; citra-rasa: Viṣṇudharmottara 67; purana-graha: Matsya 94.1).
- The ground runs from white-gold at the disc to copper at the corners.
- No blue, no green, no grey, no black.

## Stroke and surface

- Straight, firm, unbroken strokes of even width, each laid once and left; they follow the rays outward from the centre.
- Hard, clear edges; nothing blurred, nothing hesitant, nothing curled.
- Burnished and glossy, like gold leaf and polished copper: spotless, with no stain or roughness (parashara-yavana: Parāśara Tantra 3.8, "spotless, glossy").

## Setting and air

- Dry, clear, hot air; no water, no mist, no cloud.
- Below the car a bare high peak or desert ground, sunlit (parashara-yavana: Yavanajātaka 1.116, lord of peaks and deserts).

## Mood

- Sovereign, steady, unhurried, just; dignity without anger and without softness (ptolemy: Tetrabiblos III.13).
